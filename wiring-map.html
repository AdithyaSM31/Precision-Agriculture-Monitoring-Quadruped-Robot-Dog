Read before you solder

Kills the Jetson

Jetson GPIO is 3.3 V and is not 5 V tolerant. The PPD42 output swings to 4.0 V and the HC-SR04 ECHO pins to 5 V. All three must go through a divider before they touch the header. Section 04.

The Jetson will not run on 5 V. Its barrel jack needs 9–19 V. Feed it from the pack, never from a 5 V regulator.

Kills the build

Do not run servo current through the PCA9685. Its V+ screw terminal and PCB traces are not rated for 15 A peaks. Servo power gets its own bus. Section 02.

Centre all 12 servos before fitting a single horn. Horns bolted on at arbitrary angles mean the first gait command drives joints past their stops and strips gears. Section 07.

01

Power chain

One pack, three branches. Everything downstream of the switch shares a single ground, established at the battery negative.

Fuse before switch, so the fuse protects the switch too.

Why the Jetson runs straight off the pack. A 3S swings 9.6–12.6 V, which sits inside the Orin Nano's 9–19 V window, so no converter is needed. Three conditions make that safe: set the alarm at 3.5 V/cell and land when it sounds; run a separate 14 AWG pair to the Jetson so servo current doesn't drag its supply down through shared wire; and fit the 1000 µF capacitor at the barrel plug.

If you free up the budget later, a 12 V buck-boost in this branch removes the brownout risk entirely.

02

Servo power bus

The PCA9685 generates the position pulses. It must not carry the current that answers them.

To the PCA9685

Signal (yellow/orange) and GND (brown/black) only, into the 3-pin headers CH0–CH11.

The V+ screw terminal stays empty. The chip runs off VCC.

To the 6 V bus

V+ (red) of all 12 servos, split six to each UBEC across two terminal blocks.

Easiest build: use servo extension leads, cut the red wire mid-span, and splice it to the bus.

Two UBECs rather than one because 12 MG996R draw roughly 5 A while walking and spike near 15 A when several joints move together. Splitting them six and six halves the current in any one wire and leaves headroom on both regulators.

04

Level shifting

Three signals leave a 5 V device and enter a 3.3 V input. Each needs a two-resistor divider. Nothing else on the robot does.

One divider per signal. Three in total.

SignalSource levelAt the JetsonHeader pin
PPD42 OUT4.0 V2.67 V7
HC-SR04 left ECHO5.0 V3.33 V31
HC-SR04 right ECHO5.0 V3.33 V33
TRIG × 23.3 V outdirect29, 32

2.67 V clears the Jetson's ~2.3 V logic-high threshold, so one divider value serves both sensors. TRIG is an input to the HC-SR04 and fires reliably at 3.3 V, so it needs no shifting.

A TXS0108E module works too, but its auto-direction circuitry is known to misread the HC-SR04 echo pulse. Four resistors are cheaper and more reliable here.

05

I²C bus

Three devices, one bus, no address conflicts. All three run at 3.3 V so no shifting is needed on SDA/SCL.

DeviceAddressVCCRole
PCA96850x403.3 V16-channel servo PWM
SGP30 (Grove)0x583.3 VTVOC + eCO₂
MPU60500x683.3 VAccel + gyro, balance

The SGP30 arrives on a Grove connector — use a Grove-to-female-jumper lead to reach the header. Run it at 3.3 V, not 5 V, so its I²C lines match the Jetson directly.

If the bus misbehaves, it is usually too many pull-ups in parallel: the Jetson has them on-board and so do both breakouts. Lifting the pull-up resistors on one breakout normally settles it.

07

Bring-up order

Each step is a gate. Do not connect the next thing until the current one measures right.

  1. Power chain alone, nothing plugged in
    Battery, fuse, switch, both UBECs. Meter the UBEC outputs for 6.0 V and the Jetson branch for 11–12.6 V. Adjust the UBECs now — a UBEC left at 12 V destroys twelve servos in one second.
  2. Jetson alone on the pack
    Confirm it boots and stays up with no other load. Fit the 1000 µF capacitor first.
  3. I²C scan
    Wire PCA9685, SGP30 and MPU6050, then run sudo i2cdetect -y -r 7. Expect 0x40, 0x58, 0x68. Fix any missing device before going further.
  4. One servo, no horn
    Plug a single servo into CH0 with its V+ on bus A. Command 90°. Confirm it holds and the UBEC stays at 6 V.
  5. All twelve to 90°, still no horns
    This is the step people skip. Every output shaft must sit at its centre position before any mechanical part touches it.
  6. Fit horns and legs at the centred position
    Assemble against the centred shafts so each joint's mechanical range straddles the servo's electrical range.
  7. Gait, robot on a stand
    Feet off the ground until the walk cycle looks right. A gait bug with the weight on is how legs break.
  8. Sensors last
    Dividers first, then PPD42 and both sonars. Verify each divider output with a meter before the wire reaches the header.
08

Grounding

  • One star point at the battery negative, downstream of the fuse and switch. Every ground returns there.
  • Jetson GND must tie to the servo ground bus. Without a shared return, the PCA9685's PWM signals have no reference and servos twitch or ignore commands.
  • Both UBEC grounds, the PCA9685 GND, the servo bus GND, all sensor grounds, and each divider's lower resistor meet at that point.
  • Keep the 14 AWG servo pair physically away from I²C and sensor wiring. Switching current next to an unshielded I²C pair is a common source of intermittent bus errors.
  • Use 14 AWG for anything carrying servo current, 22 AWG for signals.
